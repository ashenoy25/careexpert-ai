## 1. Overview

The **National Strategy to Support Family Caregivers** was developed pursuant to the RAISE Family Caregivers Act (P.L. 115-119, 2018) and the Supporting Grandparents Raising Grandchildren Act (SGRG, P.L. 115-196, 2018). The strategy was initially released in September 2022 by the RAISE Family Caregiving Advisory Council and the Advisory Council to Support Grandparents Raising Grandchildren, with ACL serving as the coordinating federal agency.

The strategy represents the first-ever national, cross-sector plan to recognize and support the more than 53 million Americans who serve as family caregivers. It addresses caregiving across all ages and conditions -- from parents caring for children with disabilities to adult children caring for aging parents to grandparents raising grandchildren.

**Mandate:** Identify actions that federal, state, tribal, and local governments, as well as the private sector, can take to support family caregivers.

## 2. The Five Goals of the National Strategy

### Goal 1: Increase Awareness of and Outreach to Family Caregivers
- **Objective:** Ensure family caregivers are identified and recognized across all settings.
- **Key Actions:**
  - Develop public awareness campaigns about the value and challenges of family caregiving
  - Improve caregiver identification in healthcare settings (hospitals, primary care, emergency departments)
  - Expand outreach to underserved and diverse caregiver populations
  - Create culturally and linguistically appropriate caregiver resources
  - Integrate caregiver identification into social determinants of health screening
- **Practical Impact for Caregivers:** Earlier identification leads to earlier access to support services; reduces the "invisible caregiver" problem.

### Goal 2: Advance Partnerships and Engagement with Family Caregivers
- **Objective:** Include family caregivers as essential partners in health and long-term care delivery.
- **Key Actions:**
  - Engage caregivers in care planning and care transitions
  - Improve communication between healthcare providers and caregivers
  - Include caregivers in quality improvement and patient safety initiatives
  - Support caregiver involvement in hospital discharge processes
  - Develop shared decision-making models that include caregivers
- **Practical Impact for Caregivers:** Caregivers are treated as members of the care team rather than bystanders; improved care coordination and reduced medical errors.

### Goal 3: Strengthen Services and Supports for Family Caregivers
- **Objective:** Ensure family caregivers have access to an array of person-centered, trauma-informed services.
- **Key Actions:**
  - Expand respite care availability and affordability
  - Increase access to evidence-based caregiver support programs (e.g., REACH, SAVVY, BRI Care Consultation)
  - Improve caregiver training in medical/nursing tasks
  - Expand access to mental health services for caregivers
  - Support technology solutions for caregiving (telehealth, remote monitoring, care coordination tools)
  - Strengthen the National Family Caregiver Support Program (NFCSP)
  - Improve access to home and community-based services (HCBS)
- **Practical Impact for Caregivers:** More accessible and affordable support services; reduced caregiver burden through evidence-based interventions.

### Goal 4: Ensure Financial and Workplace Security for Family Caregivers
- **Objective:** Mitigate the financial impact of caregiving on families.
- **Key Actions:**
  - Expand paid family and medical leave policies
  - Support flexible workplace policies for caregivers
  - Address caregiving's impact on Social Security benefits and retirement security
  - Expand tax credits and deductions for caregiving expenses
  - Reduce out-of-pocket costs for caregivers
  - Address the "caregiver penalty" in career advancement and earnings
  - Support self-directed services that allow compensation for family caregivers
- **Practical Impact for Caregivers:** Reduced financial strain; better ability to maintain employment while caregiving; improved long-term financial security.

### Goal 5: Expand Data, Research, and Evidence-Based Practices
- **Objective:** Build the evidence base for effective caregiver supports and improve data collection.
- **Key Actions:**
  - Improve national data collection on family caregiving (prevalence, economic impact, health outcomes)
  - Fund research on caregiver interventions and their effectiveness
  - Develop and disseminate evidence-based caregiver support programs
  - Study the impact of caregiving on diverse populations
  - Improve measurement of caregiver outcomes in clinical trials and health services research
  - Evaluate the effectiveness of technology-based caregiver support tools
- **Practical Impact for Caregivers:** Better-designed programs based on evidence; improved understanding of what works for different caregiver populations.

## 3. Key Federal Programs Supporting Family Caregivers

### 3.1 ACL-Administered Programs

| Program | Description | Key Services |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **National Family Caregiver Support Program (NFCSP)** | Funded under OAA Title III-E; administered through state/area agencies on aging | Information, assistance, counseling, respite care, supplemental services, caregiver training |
| **Lifespan Respite Care Program** | Grants to states to develop coordinated respite care systems | Respite care coordination, training respite providers, emergency respite, voucher programs |
| **Eldercare Locator** | National information and referral service | Connects caregivers to local services (1-800-677-1116 or eldercare.acl.gov) |
| **Alzheimer's Disease Programs Initiative (ADPI)** | Grants for dementia-capable systems | Dementia caregiver support, care planning, community-based services |
| **No Wrong Door (NWD)** | Streamlined access to long-term services and supports | Single entry point for LTSS information, assessment, and navigation |
| **National Adult Protective Services (APS)** | Programs addressing elder abuse, neglect, and exploitation | Investigation, intervention, and prevention services |

### 3.2 Other Federal Programs Referenced in the Strategy

| Agency/Program | Relevance to Caregivers |
|---------------|------------------------|
| **VA Caregiver Support Program** | Stipends, training, respite for caregivers of veterans (Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers -- PCAFC) |
| **Medicaid HCBS Waivers** | Fund home and community-based services that support caregiving (some states allow caregiver compensation) |
| **Medicare** | Covers some home health services, caregiver training post-discharge, telehealth |
| **SAMHSA** | Mental health and substance use services relevant to caregiver well-being |
| **DoL FMLA** | Job-protected leave for caregiving (unpaid; 12 weeks/year for eligible employees) |
| **SSA** | Caregiver credits and Social Security implications |
| **HRSA** | Training programs for healthcare workforce, including caregiver engagement |

## 4. Eldercare Locator -- Key Resource

- **URL:** https://eldercare.acl.gov/
- **Phone:** 1-800-677-1116 (Monday-Friday, 9am-8pm ET)
- **What It Provides:**
  - Connection to local Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs)
  - Information about state and local caregiver support services
  - Help finding respite care, home care, transportation, meals
  - Benefits counseling and enrollment assistance
  - Long-term care ombudsman referrals
  - Legal assistance referrals
  - Housing and home modification resources
- **Language Support:** Available in English and Spanish; interpreter services for 150+ languages
- **Mapping to Caregiver Needs:** Serves as the single most important federal referral point for local services; essential resource for any caregiver seeking community-based support.

## 5. Evidence-Based Caregiver Interventions Referenced in the Strategy

The National Strategy highlights several evidence-based programs that have demonstrated effectiveness:

| Program | Target Population | Key Components |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|
| **REACH II (Resources for Enhancing Alzheimer's Caregiver Health)** | Dementia caregivers | Multi-component intervention: education, skills training, stress management, social support |
| **SAVVY Caregiver** | Dementia caregivers | Psychoeducational program teaching caregiving skills and self-care |
| **BRI Care Consultation** | Caregivers of persons with chronic conditions | Telephone-based care coordination and problem-solving |
| **TCARE (Tailored Caregiver Assessment and Referral)** | All family caregivers | Standardized assessment leading to individualized care plans for caregivers |
| **Powerful Tools for Caregivers** | All family caregivers | Self-care education program; 6-week evidence-based class series |
| **Stress-Busting Program** | Dementia and chronic illness caregivers | Multi-component stress management and coping skills program |
| **STAR-C (Staff Training in Assisted living Residences -- Caregivers)** | Dementia caregivers | Behavioral management training for in-home caregivers |

## 6. Key Statistics and Context from the Strategy

- Over **53 million Americans** serve as unpaid family caregivers (AARP/NAC 2020 study).
- Family caregivers provide an estimated **$600 billion+** in unpaid care annually (some estimates exceed $470 billion as of the 2020 study, with inflation-adjusted figures higher).
- **1 in 5** Americans is a caregiver at any given time.
- **61%** of family caregivers are also employed, with many reducing hours or leaving the workforce.
- Caregivers experience higher rates of depression, anxiety, and physical health problems compared to non-caregivers.
- **Diverse populations** face disproportionate caregiving burdens: communities of color, rural caregivers, LGBTQ+ caregivers, and those with lower incomes.
- **Grandparents raising grandchildren:** approximately 2.7 million grandparents serve as primary caregivers for grandchildren.

## 7. Implementation and Federal Coordination

### 7.1 RAISE Family Caregiving Advisory Council
- Established by the RAISE Act; comprises caregivers, healthcare providers, employers, state/local officials, researchers, and advocates.
- Provides recommendations to HHS Secretary.
- Monitors implementation progress.

### 7.2 Advisory Council to Support Grandparents Raising Grandchildren
- Established by the SGRG Act.
- Focuses specifically on the needs of kinship caregivers.
- Provides recommendations on services, benefits, and supports for grandparent caregivers.

### 7.3 Federal Interagency Working Group
- Coordinates across HHS agencies (ACL, CMS, NIH, SAMHSA, HRSA, CDC) and other departments (VA, DoL, SSA).
- Develops implementation plans and tracks progress on strategy recommendations.

### 7.4 Implementation Timeline
- **2022:** Initial strategy release with ~350 actions across federal and non-federal sectors.
- **2023-2024:** Federal agencies issued initial implementation reports; state-level strategy alignment began.
- **2024-2025:** Updated implementation progress reports; continued federal program alignment.
- **Ongoing:** Annual reporting on progress; strategy designed to be updated as new evidence and needs emerge.

## 8. ACL Recommendations Most Relevant to Technology-Based Caregiver Support

The following strategy recommendations are directly relevant to CareExpert AI and technology-based caregiver solutions:

1. **Expand technology-based support for caregivers** including telehealth, remote monitoring, care coordination platforms, and AI-assisted tools.
2. **Develop and disseminate digital resources** that are accessible, multilingual, and culturally appropriate.
3. **Support caregiver training through technology** for medical tasks, medication management, and condition-specific care.
4. **Improve interoperability** between caregiver support tools and electronic health records.
5. **Leverage the Eldercare Locator and No Wrong Door systems** as integration points for technology-based referral and navigation.
6. **Fund research on the effectiveness of technology-based interventions** for caregiver education, support, and respite.
7. **Address the digital divide** to ensure technology-based solutions are accessible to all caregiver populations, including older adults and those in rural areas.

- The five-goal framework provides a useful organizational structure for categorizing caregiver support content in the knowledge base.
