- **24/7 Helpline:** 800.272.3900 -- available around the clock in multiple languages
- **ALZConnected:** Online caregiver community and support forum
- **Community Resource Finder:** Tool to locate local dementia care resources

- Caregiving is described as encompassing a wide range of responsibilities that evolve over time as the disease progresses
- Resources are organized to address the full trajectory from early-stage through late-stage/end-of-life care

- Encouragement to seek help early and build a care team rather than doing everything alone

## 2. Daily Care

- **URL:** https://www.alz.org/help-support/caregiving/daily-care
- **Page Title:** Daily Care | Caregiving | Alzheimer's Association

### Overview

The Daily Care section provides practical, task-specific guidance for managing the everyday needs of a person living with Alzheimer's or another dementia. Topics address personal care, nutrition, activities, communication, and maintaining quality of life.

### Subtopics and Linked Pages

#### 2.1 Daily Care Plan
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/daily-care-plan
- **Key Topics:** Establishing routines and structure; adapting the plan as disease progresses; balancing independence with assistance; importance of consistency and flexibility

#### 2.2 Activities
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/activities
- **Key Topics:** Meaningful engagement and purposeful activities; adapting activities to ability level; importance of physical, social, and cognitive stimulation; creative arts, music therapy, and sensory activities; tips for reducing frustration during activities

#### 2.3 Communication and Alzheimer's
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/communication
- **Key Topics:** Changes in communication ability across stages; strategies for effective verbal and nonverbal communication; active listening techniques; managing frustration when communication is difficult; adapting communication approaches as language ability declines; use of visual cues, simple sentences, and tone of voice

#### 2.4 Food and Eating
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/food-eating
- **Key Topics:** Nutritional needs and meal planning; dealing with changes in appetite and food preferences; swallowing difficulties (dysphagia) in later stages; maintaining adequate hydration; mealtime environment and reducing distractions; finger foods and adaptive utensils; food safety considerations

#### 2.5 Art and Music
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/art-music
- **Key Topics:** Therapeutic benefits of art and music; music as a tool to reduce agitation and improve mood; art therapy approaches; how to incorporate creative activities at various stages; programs and resources for creative engagement

#### 2.6 Incontinence
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/incontinence
- **Key Topics:** Understanding causes of incontinence in dementia (both physical and cognitive); establishing toileting schedules; selecting appropriate incontinence products; skin care and hygiene; maintaining dignity during personal care; when to consult medical professionals

#### 2.7 Bathing
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/bathing
- **Key Topics:** Common challenges and resistance to bathing; safety modifications for the bathroom; step-by-step approaches to reduce anxiety; maintaining warmth and dignity; alternative bathing methods (sponge baths); adapting frequency and approach as disease progresses

#### 2.8 Dressing
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/dressing
- **Key Topics:** Simplifying clothing choices; adaptive clothing options; encouraging independence in dressing; managing resistance; laying out clothes in order; choosing comfortable, easy-on/easy-off garments

#### 2.9 Dental Care
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/dental-care
- **Key Topics:** Importance of oral health in dementia; challenges with dental hygiene compliance; finding dental professionals experienced with dementia patients; step-by-step brushing assistance; managing dentures; signs of oral pain or problems

#### 2.10 Toileting
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/toileting
- **Key Topics:** Recognizing cues that the person needs to use the bathroom; establishing regular toileting schedules; environmental adaptations (clear path, visible toilet); nighttime toileting strategies; managing accidents with sensitivity; relationship to incontinence management

#### 2.11 Sleep Issues
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/sleep-issues
- **Key Topics:** Sundowning (increased confusion and agitation in late afternoon/evening); disrupted sleep-wake cycles; creating a sleep-friendly environment; daytime activity to promote nighttime sleep; when to discuss sleep medications with physicians; managing nighttime wandering; impact of caregiver sleep deprivation

#### 2.12 Traveling
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/daily-care/traveling
- **Key Topics:** Planning trips with a person who has dementia; packing essential documents and medications; managing disruption to routine; airport and transportation considerations; knowing when travel is no longer advisable; identification and safety measures during travel

### Key Principles Across Daily Care Topics

- **Person-centered approach:** Focus on the individual's remaining abilities, preferences, and life history
- **Routine and consistency:** Predictable schedules reduce confusion and anxiety
- **Simplification:** Break tasks into manageable steps
- **Patience and flexibility:** Allow extra time; adapt when approaches are not working
- **Dignity and respect:** Maintain the person's autonomy and self-esteem as much as possible
- **Safety awareness:** Monitor for and mitigate risks in all daily activities
- **Adaptation over time:** Strategies need to evolve as the disease progresses through stages

## 3. Stages and Behaviors

- **URL:** https://www.alz.org/help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors
- **Page Title:** Stages & Behaviors | Caregiving | Alzheimer's Association

### Overview

This section covers understanding how Alzheimer's disease progresses through stages and provides guidance for managing the behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD). It helps caregivers anticipate changes and respond effectively.

### Subtopics and Linked Pages

#### 3.1 Early-Stage Caregiving
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/early-stage
- **Key Topics:** Adjusting to the diagnosis; encouraging independence while offering support; planning for the future (legal, financial, care wishes); maintaining social connections; the role of the care partner in early disease; communicating the diagnosis to family and friends

#### 3.2 Middle-Stage Caregiving
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/middle-stage
- **Key Topics:** The longest stage of the disease; increasing need for hands-on care; managing behavioral changes; adapting the living environment; building a care team and accessing support services; balancing caregiving with personal life

#### 3.3 Late-Stage Caregiving
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/late-stage
- **Key Topics:** Around-the-clock care needs; comfort care and quality of life focus; communication in late-stage (nonverbal cues, touch, presence); managing pain and discomfort; end-of-life planning and hospice; nutrition and hydration decisions; skin care and infection prevention

#### 3.4 Alzheimer's Stages (Clinical Framework)
- **URL:** /alzheimers-dementia/stages
- **Key Topics:** Preclinical Alzheimer's; Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI); Mild dementia (early-stage); Moderate dementia (middle-stage); Severe dementia (late-stage); typical progression timeline; individual variation in disease course

#### 3.5 Aggression and Anger
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/aggression-anger
- **Key Topics:** Understanding triggers for aggressive behavior; physical vs. verbal aggression; environmental and medical causes; de-escalation strategies; when to seek medical evaluation; staying safe as a caregiver; not taking aggression personally

#### 3.6 Anxiety and Agitation
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/anxiety-agitation
- **Key Topics:** Common causes (overstimulation, unfamiliar environments, pain, fatigue); calming techniques; environmental modifications; when anxiety may indicate an unmet need; pharmacological vs. non-pharmacological approaches; creating a calm, reassuring atmosphere

#### 3.7 Depression
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/depression
- **Key Topics:** Differentiating depression from apathy and dementia symptoms; signs of depression in people with dementia; treatment options (therapy, medication); impact of caregiver depression; importance of screening and professional evaluation

#### 3.8 Hallucinations
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/hallucinations
- **Key Topics:** Visual, auditory, and other sensory hallucinations; prevalence in Lewy body dementia and later stages of Alzheimer's; how to respond (do not argue or dismiss); checking for environmental triggers; when hallucinations are distressing vs. benign; medication considerations

#### 3.9 Memory Loss and Confusion
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/memory-loss-confusion
- **Key Topics:** Progressive nature of memory impairment; short-term vs. long-term memory differences; strategies for managing confusion (orientation cues, labels, routines); avoiding confrontation about memory failures; safety implications of memory loss

#### 3.10 Repetition
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/repetition
- **Key Topics:** Repeated questions and actions as a symptom of memory impairment; coping strategies for caregivers; redirecting attention; using written reminders and visual cues; understanding the emotional need behind repetition; patience techniques

#### 3.11 Sleep Changes and Sundowning
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/sleep-issues-sundowning
- **Key Topics:** Sundowning phenomenon (increased confusion, agitation, and restlessness in late afternoon/evening); disrupted circadian rhythms; strategies to manage sundowning (light therapy, structured afternoon activities, calming evening routine); sleep hygiene for people with dementia; impact on caregiver rest

#### 3.12 Suspicion and Delusions
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/suspicion-delusions
- **Key Topics:** Paranoid thoughts and false beliefs; common delusions (theft, infidelity, abandonment); understanding delusions as a symptom of the disease; not arguing with or trying to correct delusions; reassurance and distraction techniques; when to involve medical professionals

#### 3.13 Wandering
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/stages-behaviors/wandering
- **Key Topics:** Prevalence and danger of wandering; causes (disorientation, searching for something, restlessness, past routines); prevention strategies; door alarms and GPS tracking devices; MedicAlert + Safe Return program; creating a wandering response plan; community notification resources

### Key Principles Across Stages and Behaviors

- **Behavioral symptoms are communication:** Behaviors often reflect unmet needs (pain, fear, overstimulation, boredom)
- **Non-pharmacological approaches first:** Environmental modifications, routine changes, and caregiver behavior changes should be tried before medications
- **Medical evaluation:** New or worsening behaviors may indicate medical issues (infections, pain, medication side effects)
- **Stage-appropriate expectations:** Understanding what is typical at each stage helps caregivers set realistic expectations
- **Caregiver safety:** Caregivers must also protect their own physical and emotional safety
- **Individual variation:** Not everyone experiences all behavioral symptoms, and the presentation varies significantly

## 4. Caregiver Health

- **URL:** https://www.alz.org/help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health
- **Page Title:** Caregiver Health | Caregiving | Alzheimer's Association

### Overview

This section focuses on the physical, emotional, and psychological well-being of the caregiver. It recognizes that caregiving for someone with dementia is uniquely stressful and that caregiver health directly impacts care quality.

### Subtopics and Linked Pages

#### 4.1 Caregiver Stress
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/caregiver-stress
- **Key Topics:** Signs and symptoms of caregiver stress; physical health effects (compromised immunity, cardiovascular risk, chronic fatigue); emotional effects (irritability, feelings of helplessness, withdrawal); stress assessment tools; when stress becomes harmful; the importance of acknowledging stress as legitimate

#### 4.2 Caregiver Burnout
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/burnout
- **Key Topics:** Distinguishing burnout from normal stress; symptoms of burnout (emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, reduced sense of accomplishment); risk factors (lack of support, financial strain, care intensity); recovery strategies; prevention through self-care and respite; seeking professional help

#### 4.3 Caregiver Depression
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/depression
- **Key Topics:** High prevalence of depression among dementia caregivers (estimated 30-40%); symptoms to watch for; barriers to seeking help (guilt, lack of time); treatment options; the relationship between caregiver depression and care quality; screening resources

#### 4.4 Caregiver Grief and Loss (Ambiguous Loss)
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/grief-loss
- **Key Topics:** The concept of ambiguous grief -- mourning a person who is still physically present but cognitively changed; anticipatory grief; disenfranchised grief (grief that is not socially recognized); stages of grief in the caregiving context; processing loss throughout the disease journey; bereavement after death; grief support resources

#### 4.5 Caregiver Stress Check (Self-Assessment)
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/stress-check
- **Key Topics:** Self-assessment tool for evaluating stress level; prompts for reflection on physical symptoms, emotional state, and coping; guidance on interpreting results; action steps based on stress level; connection to support resources

#### 4.6 Changes to Your Relationship
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/changes-to-your-relationship
- **Key Topics:** Shifts in spousal/partner relationships; changes in parent-child dynamics; role reversal; impact on intimacy and sexuality; navigating changing family roles; maintaining connection despite cognitive decline; support for non-spousal caregivers

#### 4.7 Your Physical Health
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/caregiver-health/physical-health
- **Key Topics:** Prioritizing medical appointments and screenings; exercise and nutrition for caregivers; sleep quality and strategies; managing chronic health conditions while caregiving; recognizing when health is deteriorating; body mechanics for physical care tasks; avoiding caregiver neglect of own health

### Key Principles Across Caregiver Health Topics

- **Self-care is not selfish:** Taking care of oneself is essential for sustainable caregiving
- **Ask for and accept help:** Building a support network is critical
- **Professional support:** Counseling, support groups, and medical care for the caregiver are important
- **Respite care:** Regular breaks from caregiving are essential for preventing burnout
- **Grief is ongoing:** Loss occurs throughout the disease, not just at the end
- **Monitor your own health:** Caregivers are at increased risk for depression, cardiovascular disease, and immune suppression
- **Know your limits:** Recognizing when you cannot do it alone and transitioning to formal care services

### Support Resources Referenced

- **Support Groups:** In-person and virtual groups through local Alzheimer's Association chapters
- **ALZConnected:** Online forum for peer-to-peer caregiver support
- **24/7 Helpline:** 800.272.3900 for crisis support and information
- **Caregiver Center:** Personalized resources and care plans
- **Community Resource Finder:** Local services directory (in partnership with AARP)
- **Respite Care:** Information on finding and accessing respite services

## 5. Safety

- **URL:** https://www.alz.org/help-support/caregiving/safety
- **Page Title:** Safety | Caregiving | Alzheimer's Association

### Overview

The Safety section addresses the many safety risks that arise as dementia progresses, covering home safety modifications, wandering prevention, driving concerns, emergency preparedness, and more.

### Subtopics and Linked Pages

#### 5.1 Home Safety
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/home-safety
- **Key Topics:** Room-by-room safety assessments; fall prevention (remove trip hazards, install grab bars, improve lighting); kitchen safety (stove guards, sharp object storage, spoiled food monitoring); bathroom modifications; stairway safety; securing hazardous materials (cleaning products, medications, firearms); fire safety; home safety checklists

#### 5.2 Wandering
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/wandering
- **Key Topics:** Statistics on wandering danger (critical if not found within 24 hours); risk assessment; prevention strategies (door locks, alarms, camouflaged exits); GPS and tracking technologies; identification bracelets and clothing labels; creating a wandering response plan; working with local law enforcement; neighborhood notification; MedicAlert + Alzheimer's Association Safe Return program

#### 5.3 MedicAlert + Safe Return
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/medicalert-safe-return
- **Key Topics:** 24/7 emergency response service; nationwide wandering response network; enrollment process and identification products; how the system works when someone is found; integration with law enforcement; membership information

#### 5.4 Driving
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/driving
- **Key Topics:** When driving becomes unsafe; warning signs of impaired driving ability; having the conversation about stopping driving; driving assessments and evaluations; legal considerations and reporting; alternative transportation options; emotional impact of losing driving independence; gradual vs. immediate cessation approaches

#### 5.5 Medication Safety
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/medication-safety
- **Key Topics:** Organizing and managing multiple medications; pill organizers and medication management systems; preventing missed doses or double dosing; proper storage and disposal; monitoring for side effects and drug interactions; communicating with pharmacists and physicians; when to take over medication management completely

#### 5.6 Fire Safety and Disaster Preparedness
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/fire-safety
- **Key Topics:** Fire prevention in the home; smoke detectors and fire extinguishers; evacuation planning for someone with dementia; kitchen fire risks; candle and smoking safety; natural disaster preparedness; emergency supply kits; evacuation considerations for people with cognitive impairment

#### 5.7 Abuse
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/abuse
- **Key Topics:** Recognizing signs of elder abuse (physical, emotional, financial, neglect); vulnerability of people with dementia to abuse; caregiver abuse risk factors (stress, isolation, lack of support); reporting suspected abuse; resources for caregivers at risk of becoming abusive; Eldercare Locator and Adult Protective Services

#### 5.8 Technology and Assistive Devices
- **URL:** /help-support/caregiving/safety/technology-101
- **Key Topics:** GPS tracking devices and location monitoring; smart home technology for safety; medication reminder systems; video monitoring and check-in systems; communication tools; emergency response systems (personal alarms); evaluating and selecting appropriate technology; privacy considerations

### Key Principles Across Safety Topics

- **Proactive planning:** Safety measures should be implemented before incidents occur
- **Progressive adaptation:** Safety needs increase as the disease advances; regular reassessment is essential
- **Balance safety and autonomy:** Over-restricting can reduce quality of life; find appropriate balance
- **Environmental modification:** Changing the environment is often more effective than changing the person's behavior
- **Emergency preparedness:** Have plans in place for wandering, medical emergencies, and natural disasters
- **Community involvement:** Neighbors, law enforcement, and local agencies can be part of the safety network
- **Identification always:** The person with dementia should carry/wear identification at all times
